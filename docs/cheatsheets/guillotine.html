Guillotine is a survival game. The rules that change the board — tap to collapse

Score early or die early

The lowest-scoring team is chopped every week and its whole roster hits FAAB waivers. There are no playoffs, so a player who breaks out in Week 10 is worth nothing if you were eliminated in Week 3. Draft the best Week 1 lineup, not the best season.

Nothing speculative

No stashing injured players, no snap-count ramps, no suspended players, no "he'll be great after the bye." Consistent scorers over higher-variance breakout bets — you are trying to avoid a floor, not chase a ceiling.

Byes are the format's real killer

Take late byes over early ones. With 18 teams roughly 252 players are rostered on draft day, so a Week 5 bye is covered from an empty waiver pool. By Week 13 a dozen eliminated rosters have come back and cover is easy. This is what the board's adjustment encodes.

Running backs first

Far fewer RBs than WRs can post a usable week, and 18 teams drains the pool. Two RBs before a second WR is defensible here even if it would be bad practice in redraft. Handcuffing your own RB matters more than usual — a lost starter is not replaceable from waivers.

QB: the sources disagree

Fantasy Life says wait — take the value late and exploit early matchups. DraftSharks says a top-tier QB is a weekly floor you can bank. Both are defensible; the board does not pick for you, it just doesn't discount QBs the way a scarcity model would.

FAAB: hold your budget

Keep roughly 70–75% for the genuine difference-makers that fall from eliminated rosters, and 25–30% for the weekly patching. Get aggressive late, once you are one of the few teams left and every week is nearly a coin flip.
